Opening

On March 1, 2026 I put $15,000 into a copy-trading tool I had taken the time to understand. It places the trades. I do not. The account compounds at 0.73% a day. On November 20 it stood at $102,335. The gain is $87,335.

The only thing I do along the way is hit reinvest when I feel like it. That folds the day’s gain back in, so the 0.73% compounds instead of sitting still. There is no schedule for it, and there is no chart I have to master. The tool does the work.

Why I am sharing it

The tool does the work. I just reinvest.

Copy trading, for me, is no longer something I study. I found a tool that follows a strategy I trust, and it does the daily work. My part is small. I started with $15,000, and when I feel like it I hit reinvest, so the gains compound instead of sitting still.

People near me asked whether this was real, and whether they could do something similar. The honest answer is the page you are reading. It has been running since March, compounding at 0.73% a day, and it is ahead by $87,335. The loud version of copy trading is not the one I have been living with. This one is a tool, a starting amount, and a button I press when I want the gain to stay in.

If you can start with money you can leave in place, the habit after that is a light one. Hit reinvest when you want the day’s gain to compound into the next one. Take your time before you fund anything. A rate like this is a record of what my account has done, and records are not promises.

I wrote this so I could hand someone one page, instead of telling the story differently every time.

This is a personal record. It is not advice, and it is not an offer to manage anyone else’s money. Copy trading can lose money, including the sum you start with. A daily rate can stop, shrink, or reverse. Past performance is not a guide to what happens next. If you try something similar, read the risks of the platform you choose, and use money you do not need for anything else.
